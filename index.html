01

The Hook and Core Philosophy

Shifting our mindset from grammar-first to vocabulary-first — before we ever plan another lesson.

Objective · Reframe how we think about fluency
10 MIN
“
Language is grammaticalised lexis, not lexicalised grammar.
Michael Lewis · The Lexical Approach

The Explanation

Vocabulary came first. Grammar came after.

Humans communicated through phrases long before anyone wrote a rule down. Grammar was invented later, to categorize spoken language — not to generate it.

Vocabulary, no grammar
✅ Can communicate
Perfect grammar, no vocabulary
❌ Cannot communicate
Turn & Talk · 2 Minutes

"Have you ever met someone with amazing vocabulary but broken grammar — and understood them perfectly? What happened?"

Objective · See chunks in the wild

Our Methodology

Off-site clubs are immersive experiences, not classrooms.

Every location we choose is a stage for real, unscripted English — the classroom walls simply aren't part of the plan.

🐾

Real stakes

Students speak because they need to — to hold a puppy, order food, or pick a song — not because a worksheet told them to.

🗺️

Location-driven

The environment dictates the language. We don't teach grammar and hope it's useful — we teach exactly what the place demands.

💬

Chunk-first

Every club is built backward from 3–5 lexical chunks a student will genuinely need in that exact setting.

03

Deep Dive: What Are Lexical Chunks?

Defining the concept clearly, and seeing exactly how it differs from the grammar-first approach.

Objective · Build a shared definition
15 MIN

Definition

A chunk is one unit, not four words.

Lexical chunks are groups of words the brain processes as a single unit of meaning — retrieved whole, bypassing grammatical analysis entirely.

Stored as one piece
I've been meaning to ask you...

The Contrast

Word-by-word vs. chunk-by-chunk.

Traditional Approach

I+have+been+learning

Assembled word-by-word, rule-by-rule — slow under pressure.

→

Lexical Chunk Approach

I've been learning...+[topic]+for [X] years

Retrieved whole and dropped into a slot — instant and natural.

Categories

Chunks come in families.

Not every chunk does the same job. Knowing the categories helps us plan a balanced club.

Greetings & Openers

  • Long time no see!
  • How's it going?
  • Fancy seeing you here.

Transitions & Fillers

  • Speaking of which...
  • Anyway, moving on...
  • That reminds me...

Polite Requests

  • Would you mind if...?
  • Could I possibly...?
  • Do you happen to have...?

Active Participation: Designing the Lexical Club

Now it's your turn — teams practice designing an off-site club anchored by lexical chunks.

Objective · Build a club, not a lesson plan
20 MIN

The Challenge

You're planning next month's club.

Form teams of 3–4. You're not writing a grammar lesson — you're designing an experience.

  • 1Pick a location or activity your students haven't visited yet.
  • 2Choose 3 to 5 core lexical chunks that location will naturally demand.
  • 3Build the club around those chunks — not the other way around.

Need Inspiration?

A few starter locations.

Feel free to invent your own — the stranger the setting, the richer the chunks.

Outdoor Activity: The Chunk Hunt & Talk

Movement and a change of scenery help cement vocabulary and lower the affective filter — let's feel it ourselves.

Objective · Learn by walking, not sitting
20 MIN

Your Materials

One card. Three chunks.

Each teacher receives an index card with three functional chunks to weave naturally into conversation.

Your card
Speaking of...
I couldn't agree more.
To be honest...

The Walk

Talk while you move.

Step outside the seminar room. Keep a natural conversation going on any light topic — and seamlessly drop in all three of your chunks before we return.

  • 1Pair up with someone outside your design team.
  • 2Pick a light topic — weekend plans, favorite food, anything.
  • 3Use all three chunks on your card, naturally, before time's up.

Walking Time

Head outside now.

Start the timer as you step out. Come back when it hits zero.
